A reviewer could approve stale-browser behavior—or add broadcasts to low-level entity creation, causing mid-turn/per-entity broadcast storms.

## Monkey-baiting concentration verdict

The concentration succeeds only in the literal sense: native, simulator, threading, balance-loop, test, engine-host, and WASM knowledge is present somewhere in the ancestor. It fails as review context. Relevant sections begin around lines 136, 702, 1035, and 1303, separated by hundreds of lines of chronology, measurements, commands, and status; the wrong system and test counts show the maintenance consequence.

Keep a short root file for genuinely cross-cutting contracts—data authority, oracle/version rules, identity, draw semantics—and split review notes at the major boundaries: `systems/native`, `sim`, `tools/mb_loop`, and `clients/wasm`. The production solver also needs context under `web/monkey-baiting/solver`, because it is outside that subtree.
